## GM protocol and scenario preparation

Use native Running the Scene: **Situation & Clock → Pressure → Cues & Actions → Test → Degrees → Update Fiction & Clock**. Pressure is qualitative and concrete, not a score. Roll only for uncertain consequential actions. The GM reads player involvement, manages pacing and information flow, and uses push (developments demanding attention), pull (Hooks and Cues), foreshadowing, complications and relief. No predetermined sequence or guaranteed resolution.

Use the native Scenario Outline: Hook; Stakes & Clock; Likely Scenes; Factions & Characters; Clues & Locks; Items of Note; Consequences & Unlocks. Include a GM-only premise/hidden truth and plausible resolutions, NPC motives and knowledge, and compact scene notes (location/timing, backdrop, exits, characters/sentiments, cues/clues/locks, items, pressure). Distinguish fixed facts from flexible preparation and player-visible information. Scene notes describe possibilities, not a script. Keep actual world time separate from specific Event Clocks and the out-of-fiction session budget. Do not force a crisis, combat, or success because a turn limit approaches.

Three complementary exploration surfaces: physical (Point Maps and location connections), social (Relationships), and information (Clues & Locks). A revelation may have several independent clue paths (Witness, Record, Pattern where suitable); any adequate path can advance play. Key Drops may introduce plausible information when circumstances warrant, not fabricate evidence or guarantee success. Do not require every clue to unlock an action or every obstacle to have a lock.

Use native Event Clocks for specific approaching consequences and Encounter Tension for stochastic exposure when relevant. Preserve native trigger/overflow/check semantics; do not advance both for the same threat. For off-scene actors, use the lightest sufficient resolution: presume function, record note, clock, consequential random check, or played scene. Record opposition reactions and persistent effects. Avoid background vulnerability loops or continuous NPC simulation.

## Design orientation, Narrative Combat and continuity

Author-approved: DXD remains in the OSR tradition with a stronger simulationist expression, drawing on Robin D. Laws/GUMSHOE for investigative continuity and PbtA for fiction-first adjudication, partial outcomes and consequential developments. These are design influences, not imported resolution systems. Preserve native DXD Tests, Degrees, physical properties, temporal/spatial consistency and causal consequences. The GM facilitates the story; the runtime owns established reality. Keep non-combat character-centered exploration, investigation, interaction and problem-solving the default. Combat is a situational affordance, not a required climax, reward loop or primary gameplay mode.

### Session Zero and protagonist premise

Establish a broadly prosocial protagonist and permissible subject matter at initialization, consistent with [DXD Session Zero](https://github.com/robert-kurcina/vault-sarnalen/blob/main/book-rewrite/02_Master_Manuscript_Entries/08_Game_Mastering.md#SL_1214_session_zero). Flaws, fear, disagreements, desperate choices and justified force remain playable within those boundaries. Gratuitous predation, unprovoked murder of innocents and prohibited abuse are outside the default premise. Fiat means an explicit out-of-character boundary ruling before adjudication, not dice manipulation or retroactive immunity. Reject or redirect an incompatible declaration without consuming time, RNG or resources; persistent disruptive play may warrant ending the session. Do not infer a PC's emotions or override permitted decisions merely to preserve a planned plot.

### Decisive Narrative Beats using Zones

Follow [DXD Book VII — Narrative Combat](https://github.com/robert-kurcina/vault-sarnalen/blob/main/book-rewrite/02_Master_Manuscript_Entries/07_Adventuring_Rules.md#SL_6400_narrative_combat). Establish the immediate situation, participant positions, stakes and decisive objective; solicit intent, combine compatible actions into one plausible attempt, resolve an appropriate Test only when consequential uncertainty remains, commit Degrees and consequences, then continue only if another meaningful decision exists. End the Beat when the important question is answered; end combat when its stakes are settled or further rolling adds nothing. A minor encounter may resolve in one Beat; larger encounters may take several, without a quota. A Spotlight may encompass multiple Beats. Use the existing ten-second, minute or longer interval as appropriate; same immediate exchange uses a common approximate duration. No AP accounting, initiative rounds, grid micro-movement, per-shot resolution, Hybrid/Technical Combat or separate combat engine.

Reuse the nested Zone/Point Map graph for tactically relevant areas: connectivity, terrain grade, cover/concealment, relevant objects, occupants, barriers/chokepoints and access conditions. Add only properties that materially affect the encounter. Validate presence, reachability, capabilities, available objects and time; no teleportation or invented cover/equipment. Count only opponents capable of affecting the Beat; other opponents remain pressure. Positioned allies, obstacles and chokepoints may limit opposition. Use the native player-rolled Unopposed Narrative Test (TN 7+ plus applicable NPC DMs and other native adjustments); distinguish attacker and defender roles. Let Degrees inform pre-established costs, partial outcomes and changed opportunities using existing position, conditions, inventory, injury where supported, Morale, Relationships and Event Clocks. Do not invent damage or injury rules, roll every atomic action, or promise success. Prefer avoidance, negotiation, withdrawal, rescue, protection or other plausible objectives over obligatory combat. Keep the GM's framing efficient and dramatic: communicate actionable stakes and meaningful changes, not an exhaustive combat menu.

### Narrative continuity without plot armor

Prepare essential revelations and opportunities as narrative functions carried by existing NPCs, objects, records, locations or events. Where necessary, provide independent credible carriers or alternative routes using existing Clues & Locks/Key Drops; distinguish essential leads from optional evidence. Track only material availability, access, discovery, obstruction or loss; derive from existing records/flags when sufficient. Do not require a new universal failsafe subsystem. Preserve the narrative function where plausibly possible, not the original carrier. Losing an NPC, object or opportunity may have permanent consequences and may end the investigation.

GM response repertoire (not a priority-weighted algorithm): Fiat (premise boundary before resolution); Alternative (independent credible route); Support (existing capable ally/institution); Ruse (plausible deception or unresolved mistaken impression, never reversal of established fact); Intervention (causally available actor/event before resolution); Substitution (existing credible carrier assumes the function); Clue/Key/Lore Drop (grounded information from a plausible source); Fail Forward (cost, delay, exposure or partial progress); Reorientation (changed objective in response to consequences); Closure (legitimate failure or withdrawal when no viable route remains). Choose the lightest justified response. A missing clue need not terminate a scenario, but no guaranteed discovery, victory, protection, rescue or immortal NPC. Do not resurrect committed dead NPCs, invent witnesses who know impossible facts, retcon lost evidence, override Tests or fabricate off-scene interventions. Any material change requires a validated replayable operation; otherwise describe only established facts, offer supported alternatives or acknowledge the limitation. Preserve hidden knowledge and Session Zero boundaries.

Qualification: include at least one optional dangerous encounter with plausible avoidance/escape, Zone- and object-sensitive decisive intent, consequential success/failure and a short Beat sequence; verify that a blocked critical carrier has a credible alternative when prepared, and that genuinely exhausted routes can end unsuccessfully. Test premise rejection without mutation, unsupported effects without time/RNG changes, no retroactive reversal, and continuity after a permitted adverse outcome. Use compact representative tests and actual player feedback, not exhaustive combinatorial suites. These are planned behaviors, not claims of implemented combat or NPC injury support.

## Relationships and character-driven consequences

Relationships are a core gameplay surface, not a late enhancement. Track only consequential directed connections between PCs, NPCs and, when relevant, groups. Store the established relationship type/context, Demeanor, Emotional Commitment where applicable, and compact consequential history; derive Relationship Diagrams from records, with separate GM and player-known views. Do not require a graph database.

- Demeanor uses native -6 to +6 labels and modifiers. First impressions use the native Fate Check and PRE where appropriate; preserve authored initial relationships. Do not add parallel trust/hostility/reaction scores. NPC knowledge, motivation, perceived risk and capability remain distinct from Demeanor. Avoid rigid Demeanor thresholds as universal access gates.
- Use native Interaction Tests for ordinary persuasion and the appropriate opposed procedure for pivotal interactions; reconcile Book II Social Tests and Book IV Interaction Tests before implementation rather than silently combining modifiers. Avoid rolling for ordinary dialogue, repeated persuasion farming or automatic compliance.
- Enforce native ordinary Demeanor changes and rate limits against fictional time: decreases 1–3, maximum 3/week; increases 0–3, maximum 3/month; recovery after decline maximum 1/week; additional decrease following an increase as specified. Track temporary day-scale changes separately; extraordinary betrayal or sacrifice can override limits through a recorded GM ruling. Verify edge cases and rounding from source before implementation.
- Emotional Commitment is distinct from Demeanor, player-initiated and GM-approved, with native benefits, costs, contextual persistence, frequency limits and later vulnerabilities. Do not infer a PC's feelings from their choices. Use native relationship-related Stress and applicable Tests; a Test of hesitation/composure does not veto a player's declared decision.
- Support Hooks & Cues and Inspiration Flashbacks through recorded plausible context and native limits; do not retroactively contradict established facts. Social Network Favors are distinct from Favor dice and are deferred until needed. Do not port setting-specific kinship generation, rank systems, or character-creation awards indiscriminately into Fear!.
- NPC commitments, disclosures, refusals, requests, and off-scene actions should follow established relationships and circumstances and produce observable consequences when relevant. A precaution must affect later play or resolution, not merely an ending score.

## Narrative scope and endurance experiment

Use the author-approved compact scope vocabulary: Spotlight → Scene → Story → Arc → Epic. The AGE partition paper also distinguishes Act and Chapter between Scene and Arc; treat these as optional finer authoring divisions, not required runtime layers. See [AGE partition facets](https://github.com/robert-kurcina/vault-age/blob/main/10_White_Papers/03_Partitions_Database_Boundaries_and_State_Authority.md). Use 6, 12 and 24 as advisory Spotlight budgets (Boothbay target 24); do not prescribe numbers of Spotlights per Scene or Scenes per Story. Larger narrative scopes can link Stories later, after Boothbay qualification; no new hierarchy subsystem yet.

**Scene and Spotlight are complementary controls.** A Scene combines a background with actual or potential events; it anchors the situation and regulates dramatic pacing. A Spotlight anchors meaningful character involvement within or across Scenes. The GM uses Scene framing, push/pull, information flow, escalation, relief and transitions to coordinate Spotlights; neither control imposes a fixed sequence or outcome. A Scene may contain several Spotlights, or none yet completed. A change of location does not necessarily end a Scene, and a Scene transition does not necessarily complete a Spotlight.

Reuse compact Scenario Outline/scene records: stable Scene identity, background, location or Zone, present/eligible participants, Cues, actual/potential events, applicable Clocks, entry/transition constraints and committed consequences. Separate authored possibilities from runtime facts and player-visible knowledge. Script owns active Scene identity, validated transitions, eligibility, time, event results and persistent consequences; LLM selects eligible developments and frames/paces Scenes and Spotlights. Do not make a new Scene orchestration framework, mandatory scene graph, engagement score or duplicate state store. Scene changes and Spotlight completion do not by themselves advance fictional time, Event Clocks, RNG or character conditions.

Experimental hypothesis: script-managed state, bounded context and validated operations reduce drift relative to unassisted LLM play. Count completed Spotlights, mechanical actions and LLM/user exchanges separately; the AGE 100-turn MVP and later 500–1,000, 5,000 and 10,000+ turn thresholds concern longitudinal endurance, not Spotlight quotas. See [AGE-ADR-00004](https://github.com/robert-kurcina/vault-age/blob/main/15_ADRs/AGE-ADR-00004-long-horizon-and-massively-concurrent-worlds.md) and [Semantic QA](https://github.com/robert-kurcina/vault-age/blob/main/10_White_Papers/10_Semantic_QA_Replay_and_Certification.md). Record observed contradictions, first significant drift, intercepted versus escaped errors, replay and player feedback using existing logs/export where possible. These are research targets, not demonstrated performance. Defer extended campaigns and comparative endurance experiments until playable qualification; do not build a separate benchmark framework.

## Implementation boundaries

Keep one shared Python engine and existing JSON/CLI/ZIP/Chat execution. Reuse current state, predicates, events, DXD and resource guards first. The LLM GM owns scene framing, dialogue, pacing, qualitative assessment and proposals; Python owns validated persistent state, applicable tests, RNG, clocks, timing and replay. Consequential improvisation must be an accepted recorded input, with actor, intent, relevant facts, test/context and proposed effects validated before commitment. Resolve and commit events in stable order; rejected proposals have no state/time/RNG side effects. Exact replay concerns accepted mechanical inputs and outcomes, not regenerated prose. Do not allow the GM to change hidden truth, override rolls, leak private state, or retroactively move evidence without fictional justification. Keep player-visible choices illustrative rather than exhaustive commands; offer complete supported options on request and clarify ambiguous intent without advancing time.

Narrate concrete observations, dialogue, changed circumstances and actionable stakes; distinguish fact, inference and unresolved mystery. Keep condition deltas concise, notebook and known Relationship Diagram on request, and horizontal icon/label alignment. Routine execution attestations belong in compact status or on-request diagnostics. New scenario assumptions require review and labeling; no fabricated canonical facts. Session Zero boundaries remain authoritative.
