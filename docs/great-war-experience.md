# Great War experience — final-cycle design

The aim is a memorable strategic tragedy or triumph shaped by the player, not
a sequence of optimization puzzles or a predetermined historical narrative.
This is AGE-owned design guidance, not a canonical historical source.

## Dramatic contract

Each consequential choice should expose a conflict between things the player
has reason to value. A choice is not meaningful merely because a number
changes. The briefing should establish a concrete stake, a credible reason
to act now, materially different courses, and uncertainty about independent
actors. Consequences should return later as changed relationships, access,
resource availability, institutional decisions or opportunities. No outcome
is narrated as achieved until the engine records it.

Use the following authoring targets for the eight-quarter opening. These
are pressure patterns, not mandatory scripted plot beats:

| Phase | Dramatic purpose | Gameplay proof |
|---|---|---|
| Q1–2 | Establish rival interests, attachments and a contested opportunity | Two feasible approaches with different costs; restraint is viable |
| Q3–4 | Bring the consequences of earlier commitments into conflict | At least one prior choice materially changes a subsequent opportunity |
| Q5–6 | Escalate through autonomous institutional choices and resource pressure | A credible counterstrategy or independent development forces reassessment |
| Q7–8 | Convergence of earlier decisions; resolution of outstanding stakes | Distinct endings with gains, losses and unresolved consequences explained |

A quiet quarter is legitimate. Do not artificially create a crisis when
there is no supported cause, or force a betrayal, defeat or canonical ending.
The 6/12/24 Critical Decision pacing targets remain independent of quarters.

## Briefing and choice construction

Start with a short concrete incident or report grounded in actor-visible
state, then explain its strategic significance. Prefer a specific institution,
relationship, threatened commitment or opportunity over abstract global
indices. Give a small set of meaningfully different feasible approaches,
including restraint when relevant; invite a custom intention. Never
promise an unsupported action or invent odds.

The matrix decision brief now exposes opening affordability, authored
contributions, isolated goal tradeoffs, recent own resolutions, remaining
turns and observed changes. An isolated effect is NOT an outcome forecast.
Show the player the price paid even when the net result is unchanged.
Unseen counterpart orders remain private. The LLM may interpret dramatic
significance but may not manufacture ledger changes.

Illustrative, noncanonical choice: an independent council is deciding whether
to maintain a contested transit route. Protecting the route might preserve
access but exhaust resources needed elsewhere; supporting local autonomy may
cost short-term influence; abstaining preserves capacity but lets other
actors determine the result. A council's acceptance cannot be assumed.
This example is a target for scenario authoring, not a claim that consent
mechanics are integrated into the matrix yet.

## Closure qualification

Run at least two distinct player strategies against the same opening and
independent opposition. Preserve the actual recorded choices and outcomes.
Review whether the player could explain the stakes before committing, whether
a later situation arose causally from an earlier decision, whether restraint
was credible, whether opposing actors remained independent, and whether
the ending reflects the player's gains and sacrifices rather than a forced
plot. These are experiential review questions, not automated scores.

The remaining concrete integration work is the private historical opening,
bounded negotiated consent where required, scenario-authored developments,
and quarter-specific presentation through the existing play adapter.
Do not create a parallel narrative engine or a generic drama scoring system.
Historical specifics stay in the AGE private workspace; vaults are read-only.
Stop expanding after Great War delivery and qualification.
